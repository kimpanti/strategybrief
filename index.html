Phase 1 · Step 3 of 4
Question & Answer
The question flows inevitably from your complication. The answer is your boldest current hypothesis.
Q — Question: What central question does the complication force you to answer? It should feel inevitable. Example: "How should we modernise our technology platform to retain enterprise clients — and at what speed and investment level?"
A — Answer / Hypothesis: Your best current belief. Bold and direct — not a hedge. This will be stress-tested. Example: "We should pursue a phased cloud migration partnering with a technology vendor, prioritising client-facing tracking first."
Q — Key question
The single question your strategy must resolve. Usually starts with "How should we…" or "What must we do to…"

Phase 1 · Step 4 of 4 · MECE Stress-Test
Are your options airtight?
MECE = Mutually Exclusive, Collectively Exhaustive. Claude is reviewing whether your options cover all realistic paths without overlapping.
Why MECE matters: Weak option sets lead to weak strategies. If options overlap, you're comparing the same thing twice. If there are gaps, you may miss the best path entirely.
